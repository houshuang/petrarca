## What should remain accessible after three months

The five substantive areas plus terms are broader than the current thirteen practice
items. Selected recorded speech establishes exposure to topics, not prior mastery.

| Area | Intended accessible knowledge |
|---|---|
| Chronology | Reconstruct older Stone Age → younger Stone Age → Bronze Age, with approximate boundaries and what the labels represent. Place developments without implying simultaneous change everywhere. |
| Ways of life and transitions | Describe a plausible hunter-gatherer livelihood; explain changes in food, settlement, work and mobility as farming spreads, what continued, and why change was uneven. |
| Landscape and geography | Explain ice, land uplift, changing shorelines and opportunities for settlement/farming; recognize differing regional patterns in north and south. |
| Technology and wider connections | Explain why stone/metal access mattered and how materials connected communities over distances; avoid interpreting a period label as universal technology adoption. Connect Norwegian developments to a couple of meaningful European developments. |
| Language, identity and evidence | Retain important questions about language/migration and distinguish artifacts, archaeological cultures and claims about identity or language; know limits of inference rather than every competing argument. |
| Terms and concepts | Recognize selected terms and have enough rough meaning to interpret a passage. Examples already introduced include marin grense, ard and svibruk. For selected culture names, aim for rough time/place associations, not exhaustive lists of traits. |

No obligation to retain every site, tool subtype, exact date, source argument or
speculative explanation. Source-check target claims, especially specific language,
migration and contact routes, before grading. Separate book-encountered knowledge
from connections introduced later by Petrarca or another source. The original twelve
broad target candidates and twenty concept candidates remain candidates; we have not
quietly made all of them memory obligations or verified them by recording this plan.

## Proposed measurement

A recorded phone assessment, with feedback withheld until the unaided and neutral-cue
stages have ended:

1. **Unaided overview:** tell the story of this period in your own words. Observe
   spontaneous retrieval and organization; omissions alone do not establish ignorance.
2. **Neutral coverage prompts:** address the five substantive areas, with the actual
   cue and order recorded. Distinguish spontaneous knowledge from knowledge available
   when asked. No answer fragments or leading hints before this stage is finished.
3. **Terms and application:** interpret selected terms in unfamiliar sentences and
   answer differently phrased questions requiring use of the framework beyond a card.

Preserve original audio and score areas separately:

| Level | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not available under the specified prompt, or substantially mistaken; retain which. |
| 1 | Fragments available; central explanation missing or confused. |
| 2 | Usable main idea with appropriate qualification. |
| 3 | Coherent explanation, also demonstrated in a new context when transfer is claimed. |

Unasked, inaudible, interrupted and otherwise unscorable evidence is missing, not
zero. Record spontaneous versus prompted performance independently of adequacy.
An eloquent response to the familiar prompt alone cannot establish transfer. Obtain
confidence before feedback and compare it to performance; do not infer confidence
from a post-answer “knew” tap. Preserve scorer, rubric/version, cited audio spans,
uncertainty and later adjudication. Independent re-rating of a subset remains part
of the proposed broader design, not a completed validation.

**Working three-month criterion:** at least level 2 in each of the five substantive
areas; useful contextual meaning for roughly eight of ten deliberately selected
terms; successful application in two of three new questions. These are practical
threshold proposals, not scientific norms or an agreed fixed exam. Select and verify
the actual terms/questions, acceptable date ranges and scoring examples before judging
outcomes. Keep the dimension-level profile visible even if a headline threshold is met.

Take a first post-reading observation soon, then comparable three-month and optional
six-month follow-ups. No reminders are created by this document. The delay anchor
must be explicit (e.g. this bounded reading batch's completion), with actual dates,
intervening reading/review and last relevant exposure stored. Whole-volume assessment
may be a different occasion. Preserve the initial pre-reading dump, but do not assign
zero to topics it never elicited. The next observation has already followed reading
and AI discussion: it is not a pristine pre-exposure baseline. Results reflect retention
under the actual ongoing learning routine, not months of unassisted decay.

## Connections during subsequent reading: an additional outcome

Stian proposes that making connections to earlier reading is an indication of success.
Treat these as observations of knowledge being used during real reading, complementary
to formal tests. They may connect to earlier pages, another volume, another book or
another country/period. A contrast, qualification or revision can be valuable; similarity
is not the only useful relationship. Merely mentioning two topics is weaker evidence
than explaining the relationship and why it matters here.

Low-burden example: “Min kobling: Dette minner meg om [earlier reading], fordi …”
If the source/page comes naturally, include it; no lookup is required while reading.
Unknown source details can be linked later or remain unresolved. This example is a
capture template, not an observed participant statement or historical claim.

For a connection episode, the desired record is:

- Current recording/session and timestamped span; current book/page if known.
- The earlier source/target invoked, with participant certainty and unresolved links.
- The relationship in the participant's original words: comparison, contrast, cause,
  chronology, concept use or revision; overlapping labels are possible.
- Whether the connection arose before a prompt, after an app question, from an explicit
  connection in the current book, or with origin unknown; record book/help state.
- Whether it helped interpret the new passage or altered the earlier understanding,
  supported by the utterance or later feedback, not assumed by the analyst.
- Historical adequacy and limits, separately assessed; later revisits/corrections and
  relevant intervening exposures when known.

**Prediction:** meaningful earlier knowledge becomes available when new reading
provides an occasion to use it. **Alternatives:** the new author supplied the entire
comparison; an app prompt cued it; the connection is superficial or mistaken; or more
connections are merely being verbalized because markers were introduced.

Do not equate a book-open connection with closed-book recall. It can still be valuable
situated integration. Distinguish spontaneous, prompted and source-supplied relationships
rather than discarding all but one kind. A spontaneous recollection of an earlier text
is evidence for that episode, not proof the complete target remains mastered.

Evaluate informative episodes and counterexamples over time, not only raw counts.
Counts depend on reading amount, opportunities, source repetition and recording/marker
habits. No marker or spoken connection is not evidence that none occurred mentally.
Do not impose a connection quota or make deliberate linking a performance requirement.
No fixed numerical connection-success threshold is set yet. This outcome complements
the provisional assessment thresholds; it does not retroactively inflate their scores.

## Optional spoken markers: make provenance easier without disrupting reading

Natural “I think,” “I wonder,” “jeg tror” and “jeg lurer på” can help, but the book may
itself contain those words. A more distinctive boundary phrase is easier to interpret:

| Intent | Optional phrase |
|---|---|
| Own thought / interpretation | “Min tanke …” / “My thought …” |
| Own question | “Jeg lurer på …” / “I wonder …” |
| Connection to earlier reading | “Min kobling …” / “This reminds me of … because …” |
| Resume reading aloud / reporting the book | “Tilbake til boka …” / “Back to the book …” |

One phrase at a change of mode is enough; no need to label every sentence. An end
marker helps bound a longer digression. Forgetting markers is normal: do not stop,
repeat the recording or invent the omitted boundary afterward. Avoid letting one
opening marker classify the remainder of the whole recording as personal speech.
When the end is unclear, retain only the confidently bounded utterance and leave
surrounding passages unresolved. If reporting a recollection later, label it as later.

Markers indicate intended authorship/function, **not correctness, confidence, novel
synthesis or retention**. A personal thought can repeat something just read. A question
can expose a useful gap without demonstrating an answer. Keep observation of the
marker separate from the analyst's attribution, and retain mixed/unknown categories.
Do not automatically classify a matching phrase inside a quotation as a personal marker.
Check original audio when an ASR error or missed boundary would affect interpretation.

Keep raw audio and every transcript version unchanged. Proposed future annotation
fields: observed marker text and span, candidate function/author, attribution basis,
start/end certainty, book/help state, annotator/version and evidence links. This is a
documented annotation design, not a deployed parser. Existing 189 unmarked segments
remain unresolved unless specific new evidence justifies a versioned correction.

Record marker-protocol adoption/changes and actual observed use by session; introducing
markers changes what is observable. Do not misread increased annotation coverage as
increased integration. If marker use feels burdensome, simplify or abandon the convention
and preserve the resulting uncertainty.
